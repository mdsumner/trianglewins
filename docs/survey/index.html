Triangulation in R: a survey of packages and where their algorithms come from

Every R package found (CRAN, GitHub, r-universe) that triangulates points, polygons or segment sets, classified by algorithm, upstream library, input, constraints and output control, with a short look at Python. Facts were checked against package source on 6 October 2026; claims resting only on upstream documentation are marked (upstream docs). No package was run for this page: measured behaviour is in the harness and the benchmark, and the three main engines are compared on worked examples in Triangulation in context. "xy" and "xyz" are coordinate dimension, and triangles and tetrahedra are topological dimension; see Notes.

Before the algorithm

For a short answer by kind of problem, see the table in Start here. What often decides the choice before the algorithm does:

  • Licence. RTriangle, fdaPDE's mesher, sfdct and anglr carry Triangle's non-commercial terms; tripack and akima the ACM licence (interp replaces both). Everything else is GPL, LGPL, MIT or MPL.
  • Installation. No system dependencies: decido, deldir, geometry, interp, RCDT, fmesher, tulpaMesh, RTriangle, terrainmeshr, cdtr. GDAL and GEOS: sf, terra, gdalraster (geos bundles GEOS via libgeos). gmp and mpfr: delaunay, cgalPolygons. Rust: trowel. RcppCGAL headers: laridae.
  • Index or geometry out. The GEOS family returns triangle polygons, so topology must be rebuilt by matching coordinates. Everything else returns vertex indices.
  • Duplicates and crossings. RTriangle refuses duplicated vertices (merge shared vertices first); CDT and CGAL packages split crossing segments; GEOS needs valid polygons.
  • Inside and outside. Ear cutting and GEOS use ring nesting; Triangle and fdaPDE need hole seed points; RCDT and delaunay use parity, which cannot express a coverage of neighbouring polygons; cdtr, trowel and laridae report depth and leave the choice to you.

1. The algorithm families

The families differ in what they promise about the output, which matters more than speed.

  • Ear cutting. Rings (with holes) in; triangles using only the input vertices out. No shape guarantee, not Delaunay, no points or loose segments. O(n^2) worst case; earcut's z-order hashing makes it fast in practice.
  • Point Delaunay. Points only; the convex hull triangulated with the empty-circumcircle property. In R: incremental insertion (Lee-Schachter, Bowyer-Watson, quad-edge), radial sweep (S-hull), Fortune's sweepline, Quickhull (any number of coordinates, giving d-simplices), Delaunator.
  • Constrained Delaunay (CDT). Points plus segments forced to be edges, otherwise as Delaunay as possible. No new vertices except, in some libraries, at crossings. Holes and outside removed afterwards by seeds, parity or nesting.
  • Conforming Delaunay. Segments split with Steiner points until every piece is a Delaunay edge.
  • Quality refinement (Ruppert, Chew, off-centres). A CDT plus Steiner points until triangles meet a minimum angle and maximum area or edge length; some add a size function or a Steiner budget.
  • Smoothing (DistMesh). Points moved by a spring analogy against a signed distance function, re-triangulated each iteration. No exact constraint edges.
  • Terrain simplification. Greedy insertion into a Delaunay TIN where a height grid's error is worst.

6. Terrain TINs

terrainmeshr::triangulate_matrix() (CRAN 1.0.1, MIT): Fogleman's hmm, Garland and Heckbert greedy insertion on a height matrix, to a maximum error or triangle budget. lasR and lidR build TINs from point clouds (section 3).

11. Observations and offers

Proposals only; nothing has been submitted.

  1. Depth is unique to this project's three packages, and is why a coverage works here and not elsewhere. CDT already has calculateTriangleDepths(): exposing it is a small offer to RCDT and PythonCDT, and CGAL's nesting level is the same offer to delaunay.
  2. The GEOS functions are often read as general constrained Delaunay. A documentation line in sf, geos, terra, gdalraster and shapely saying "polygon triangulation, no Steiner points, per polygon" would help.
  3. One CDT, three copies. RCDT and tulpaMesh vendor pre-2.0 CDT, cdtr 2.0.1. A shared header package (as BH and RcppCGAL are) would let one upgrade reach all three.
  4. tulpaMesh's combined angle and area Ruppert loop is what CDT itself lacks; upstreaming it would benefit RCDT, tulpaMesh, cdtr and PythonCDT.
  5. RTriangle could merge or report duplicated vertices rather than refuse them, and say when refinement stops short.
  6. spade exclude_faces in its refinement parameters would make trowel's re-refine cheaper and help every spade user.
  7. Harness breadth: RCDT and delaunay (same input shape), fmesher and tulpaMesh (refinement, different controls) and the GEOS polygon triangulation are the cheap additions. A backend adapter is one function in R/backends.R, which is the easiest invitation to make.
  8. Python: a core crate under trowel with a PyO3 layer would give Python an editable mesh, and the harness cases (CSV) and measures port easily, making the comparison cross-language.

Adjacent but out of scope: surface and volume meshing in xyz (Rvcg, cgalMeshes, TDA, alphashape3d), hulls (alphahull, concaveman), external programs (SAGA, GRASS, QGIS), and packages that only consume a mesh (INLA, inlabru, sdmTMB, rayrender). rgeos left CRAN in 2023.
