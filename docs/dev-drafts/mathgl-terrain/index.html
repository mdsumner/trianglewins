math.gl terrain, read from the mesh side

Development draft, 31 October 2026. A study suite, not a proposal: we read the math.gl terrain roadmap against our own mesh work and test what we can on our side first. Nothing here has been sent upstream. Studies so far: Breaklines in terrain meshes.

What math.gl is building

visgl/math.gl#341 (Ib Green, 27 October 2026) plans a terrain substrate in @math.gl/geospatial/terrain. The long form is dev-docs/terrain-roadmap.md, with tranches T0 to T7. This reading is of commit c9cc7d6 (28 October 2026).

A lot has already landed:

  • height fields with explicit cell or vertex registration, validity and a height reference;
  • tiled snapshots with halos;
  • regular meshes, plus MARTINI and Delatin adaptive meshes (Delatin moved over from loaders.gl);
  • BVH ray and contact queries;
  • slope and aspect checked against GDAL 3.9.3;
  • contours and profiles;
  • vertex-tile reconciliation, mixed-resolution seam stitching and skirts.

The next steps are a mesh lab (regular, MARTINI and Delatin on more DEMs), tile continuity, and adoption in loaders.gl, luma.gl and deck.gl. Last, and only once real consumers exist, comes T7: constrained TINs, breaklines, incremental edits, hydrologic conditioning, and metrics for finite-volume solvers.

The roadmap is careful about error. It separates measured, estimated and bounded error. It keeps source terrain, displayed geometry and simulation resolution apart. It also says plainly that "a vertical error measured at original samples does not by itself certify every point of an interpolated surface". Our first study measures exactly that.

Questions to work through here

Each question gets a study page, with code under survey/dev-drafts/mathgl-terrain/ in this repository.

  1. What does a breakline buy over sample-only meshing? Done: Breaklines in terrain meshes. On an analytic cliff and on the Hobart coast, it covers sample error, true-surface error, coastline height and triangle count.
  2. How far is sample error from surface error on real DEMs? Partly done in study 1, against the bilinear surface for Hobart. Study 1 now also runs on math.gl's own pacific and sierra-nodata fixtures (11 November). Next: MARTINI, and a polar case. The REMA ice-sheet margin (rema-ovr) is a natural breakline in EPSG:3031.
  3. Can the greedy mesher match Delatin's triangle count with constraints? The R prototype inserts points in batches and needs about 35% more triangles. A one-at-a-time version would settle whether constraints cost anything at all.
  4. Seams as constraints. Two tiles that share a boundary constraint mesh independently and still conform. How does that compare with stitching after the fact? What does edge_sources record for the shared edge?
  5. Registration in the mesh. Cell-registered DEM samples give a mesh that stops half a cell short of the tile's edge. Write this down for the GRID model, alongside their 256/257 note.
  6. Exactness. Shewchuk predicates are their CPU baseline. Where do float breakline vertices from a projected vector source break a constrained mesh, and does laridae's exact path matter for terrain at all?
  7. Interchange. Would a mesh written in the triangle-form tables (or geoarrow.triangles) carry everything TerrainGridMesh and AdaptiveTerrainMesh carry? Map field by field, including heightReference and the error record.

Not doing yet

  • No comments, issues or pull requests to math.gl, loaders.gl or deck.gl.
  • No JavaScript port of the constrained mesher. The studies use cdtr from R, and Delatin from npm (the same algorithm math.gl vendors) for comparison.
